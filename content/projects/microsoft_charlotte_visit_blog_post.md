Twelve years ago, I was an eight-year-old kid begging my dad just to drive me onto the Microsoft Charlotte campus so I could look at the buildings from the outside. Earlier this week, I stepped foot onto that exact same campus, except this time I walked right through the front doors, bringing an entire group of students from UNC Charlotte with me. Working at Microsoft has always been a massive dream of mine, and experiencing that environment firsthand while leading a student organization felt nothing short of surreal.

## The Setup

When we founded Microsoft Learn at UNCC alongside Rashid Cherdabayev, our goal was never just to host another lecture series on campus. We wanted to build bridges between student ambition and industry reality. Putting together an off-campus trip of this magnitude during our very first semester felt ambitious, maybe even a little crazy, especially when partnering up with another powerhouse organization like Girls Who Code at UNC Charlotte. 

Pulling this off took an incredible amount of behind-the-scenes grit. Between Jeffery Ochie spearheading the logistics, our executive board (Joshalin Rivera M, Angelina Mikhail, Benas Markauskas, Raey Assefa, and Cristian Flores) keeping every detail on track, and the phenomenal collaboration from Girls Who Code leaders Mariam Sawires and Wannie Zarwolo, we turned an ambitious idea into a chartered bus full of eager engineers. With Jay Skipworth facilitating our transport, we were officially on our way.

## Inside the Gates

From the moment we arrived, the energy was electric. We were welcomed by Segun Akinyemi, whose passion for his work and genuine drive to uplift students set the tone for the entire day. He delivered a presentation that cut straight through the noise of typical career advice, followed by an inside look at the campus where we got to see Microsoft's culture in action.

![UNC Charlotte students visiting the Microsoft Charlotte campus](/images/blog/microsoft-charlotte-visit/group-photo.png "Microsoft Learn at UNCC and Girls Who Code at the Microsoft Charlotte office")

We also had the privilege of sitting down and talking with a panel of professionals across totally different domains: Alexandra Jones, Chinette Stevenson, Tod Edwards, Sai Nitesh, Deepali Khandekar, Sankar Muthuvelayutham, and Pauline Robinson, MHA, PMP, Prosci. Hearing them break down their day-to-day workflows, how they navigate massive technical challenges, and what they actually look for in early-career talent was priceless.

:::tip Takeaway
"Certifications give you curriculum. Projects get you hired." — Segun Akinyemi
:::

## What I Learned

When Segun dropped that line about certifications versus projects, you could practically feel the room shift. As students, it is so easy to fall into the trap of collecting badges, acing exams, and following tutorials step-by-step because it feels safe and measurable. 

But tutorials don't teach you how to debug a broken deployment at 2:00 AM or how to design software for real human beings. Moving beyond theory and building messy, real-world projects is the single biggest differentiator in tech. It proves you don't just know the syntax; you know how to solve problems.

Beyond the technical advice, I learned the true power of showing up and building community. Looking around the room and seeing my peers asking sharp questions, making connections, and realizing that they belong in spaces like Microsoft reminded me why we worked so hard to start this club in the first place. Eight-year-old me just wanted to see the building. Today, I realized the best part of getting inside the room is holding the door open for everyone else.
