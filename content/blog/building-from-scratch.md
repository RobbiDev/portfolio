Most student organizations start with a sign-up sheet and good intentions, then fade into a quiet Discord server by midterms. When Rashid Cherdabayev and I sat down to map out Microsoft Learn at UNCC, we made a pact: if we were going to build a tech club from scratch, it had to actually change the trajectory of the students who walked through our doors.

## The Setup

UNC Charlotte has no shortage of talented, driven computer science students. What was missing was a bridge between the theory taught in lecture halls and the reality of modern software engineering. Students could pass a data structures exam, but many felt lost when it came to cloud architecture, shipping real products, or getting in front of people who work in the industry.

We didn't want another resume-filler club that ran a generic slide deck once a month. We wanted an organization built on momentum, hands-on building, and direct access to industry.

Starting from zero meant a lot of unglamorous work:

- Navigating university paperwork to get recognized at all.
- Pitching the vision to anyone who would listen.
- Convincing busy students that a brand-new organization was worth an hour of their week.

## What Happened

### We built the team first

You can't scale a vision alone. The most important decision we made early was recruiting an executive board that cared as much about execution as we did. Bringing on Jeffery Ochie, Joshalin Rivera M, Angelina Mikhail, Benas Markauskas, Raey Assefa, and Cristian Flores turned a rough blueprint into a machine that ran.

### We treated semester one like a launch

Instead of spending a year "getting established" before attempting anything big, we ran our first semester like a startup launch, built on three pillars.

1. **Relentless execution.** Every meeting had to be worth the time. If students gave us an hour, they left with a new technical skill, a direction for a project, or a connection in the industry.
2. **Radical collaboration.** Rather than compete with established clubs for attention, we partnered with them. Working alongside Girls Who Code at UNC Charlotte multiplied our reach and brought more perspectives into every room.
3. **Aiming high, early.** We didn't settle for small campus room bookings. In our very first semester, the team handled the logistics, transport, and corporate outreach to take a busload of UNCC students inside the Microsoft Charlotte office.

> People don't join organizations for a logo. They join for momentum.

:::tip Takeaway
Give a capable team real ownership, and you can fit a year's worth of growth into a single semester.
:::

## What I Learned

Starting Microsoft Learn at UNCC taught me that leadership has very little to do with having all the answers. At first I thought my job as a founder was to plan every detail myself. I learned quickly that the real job is to set a clear, ambitious standard, clear the roadblocks, and trust the team to run with it.

The most rewarding part of my time in college has been watching students who were once intimidated by the tech industry stand inside corporate offices, build cloud projects, and find their people.

Build the room you wish had existed when you were a freshman, and the right people will show up to fill it.
